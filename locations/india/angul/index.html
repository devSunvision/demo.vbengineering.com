The practice

The engineering decisions the Angul practice is built to solve.

Brownfield Digital Engineering is not a service line - it is a posture. Below are the four decision-pillars the Angul practice carries.

DC arc flash on aluminium pot lines

IEEE 1584.1 method. Pot line current up to 400 kA. We compute incident energy at the busbar, the rectifier transformer interface, and the bus tie. Bilingual labelling. PPE matrix tuned to the actual cal/cm-squared envelope - not the AC default.

AC-side rectifier transformer coordination

The rectifier transformers tie the AC evacuation grid to the DC pot line. Coordination across this interface is non-trivial. We model under ETAP with the harmonic injection profile derived from the rectifier converter datasheet.

Brownfield modernisation HAZOP

Smelter modernisations - pot line upgrades, ESP retrofits, lining changes - are HAZOP triggers under IEC 61882. We run HAZOP, LOPA and SIL where required, with Principal Reviewer FIE signature.

Coal-haulage discrete event simulation

Coalfield-to-smelter haulage modelled in FlexSim. Train loadout cycle time, haul truck fleet productivity, stockpile management. Capex case for haul fleet expansion validated under stochastic demand.

Why brownfield is different

Brownfield is not greenfield with old equipment.

DC arc flash analysis is the sharpest test of an engineering practice. The math is harder than AC. The standards are newer. The plant data is rarer. The Angul practice carries the IEEE 1584.1 method, signs at the FIE level, and hands back a defensible incident energy picture for the pot line - which is the single most operator-relevant safety question in a smelter.

Engineering integrity

What we won't do.

A Chartered Engineering practice is defined as much by what it refuses as by what it delivers. These are the four lines we hold.

01

No published price

Scope drives the proposal. Every engagement is scoped per facility and quoted in writing within 5 business days. There is no rate card on this site.

02

No AI-generated cinematic plant imagery

Photographs that appear on engineering pages are real plant work the practice has been part of. We do not synthesise refinery skylines or fictional control rooms to look impressive.

03

No drawings without a Chartered Engineer signature

Every drawing, study and report that leaves the practice carries two signatures - the drafting Practice Lead and an independent Principal Reviewer. This is not negotiable.

04

No spec drift after the proposal is signed

If the scope changes mid-engagement, we re-scope and re-quote. Hidden scope creep is not how a Chartered Engineering practice operates.
